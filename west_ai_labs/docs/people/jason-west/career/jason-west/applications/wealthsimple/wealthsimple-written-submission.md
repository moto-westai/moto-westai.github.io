# Wealthsimple AI Builder — Written Submission
**Applicant:** Jason West | West AI Labs
**Deadline:** March 2, 2026

---

I run West AI Labs — a one-person IT consulting operation. Twelve days ago I built Moto: an autonomous AI agent running 24/7 on bare-metal Ubuntu.

---

**What can the human now do that they couldn't before?**

Parallel everything. Before Moto, complex technical work demanded my full attention — one problem at a time. SSH into a server? Heads-down for an hour, unreachable. Now I describe intent in natural language and Moto executes: it plans, delegates to sub-agents, monitors, and reports back while I stay in the conversation.

Today alone: Moto tore down GCP infrastructure via Terraform, provisioned a sibling AI on a Mac Mini M4 Pro, built five new operational skills, and updated its own rules — while I answered emails. It manages job search pipelines for two people simultaneously, publishes blog content, scrapes the web without API keys, and runs local LLMs at zero cloud cost (Qwen2.5-32B on local hardware).

One person. Five parallel workstreams. I'm not faster — I'm multiplied.

---

**What is AI responsible for?**

Orchestration, memory, and monitoring — the cognitive overhead of "what needs to happen next, and did it happen?" That used to live in my head. Now it lives in Moto's pipeline.

AI handles execution of anything I can describe precisely enough. Infrastructure ops, content pipelines, research, documentation. Work that used to require a team of three.

---

**Where must AI stop?**

Judgment calls. Every morning Moto surfaces what it did and flags what it couldn't decide. That line — the "should I?" question — is mine.

I don't let AI decide whether to terminate a contract, escalate a client situation, or override a human call. Not because AI can't generate an answer, but because accountability can't be delegated. When something breaks, a human owns it.

I also don't let Moto rewrite its own memory without review. It proposes. I approve. Trust is earned incrementally — that's not a technical constraint, it's a design principle.

---

**What would break first at scale?**

Context drift. At two people, I know what Moto doesn't know. At two thousand, nobody does. The model fills gaps with plausible answers. Persistent memory helps — Moto learns across sessions via files — but unreviewed memory becomes noise. The failure mode isn't catastrophic collapse; it's slow, invisible divergence from intent, discovered six weeks later when someone asks why the system made a call.

The fix: audit trails and human checkpoints at decision forks. Not optional at scale.

---

The vision behind West AI Labs is straightforward: what I built in 12 days on spare hardware should be accessible to any small business owner who can't afford an ops team. Not a SaaS wrapper — actual autonomous infrastructure, owned and operated.

I'm 55, a Navy veteran, 25 years in IT. I've spent my career watching enterprise technology overcomplicate simple problems.

Moto is the opposite of that.

The question isn't whether AI changes work. It already has. The question is who builds the systems that make it real for everyone else.
